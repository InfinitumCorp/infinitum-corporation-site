01 / IN PLAIN TERMS

What it is.

Prices react to stories, and stories react to prices. That loop is called reflexivity. Today it runs faster than ever: social feeds, trading algorithms, analysts and AI summaries all pick up a narrative, act on it and pass it on.

A small, well-placed story that a company is risky can raise its cost of capital, slow an IPO, stall a product launch or drive talent away, whatever its fundamentals say. RCW™ names that pattern, explains how it works and gives leaders a framework to compete and defend within it.

RCW™ extends Infinitum's Corporate Cognitive Warfare work into capital markets. It was developed by our founder and president, William Aaron Turner.

02 / KEY IDEAS

Four ideas to know.

  • RiskFab™

    Risk fabrication: building a picture of a company, product or leader as risky or in decline. It rarely needs false facts. Selective emphasis, missing context and small issues framed as big ones are usually enough.

  • DoubtSprout™

    Seeding persistent doubt among investors, customers, employees and regulators at the same time. Once doubt takes root, even accurate answers can sound defensive, and decisions get delayed.

  • Guerrilla Economics

    Small, scattered, hard-to-trace pressure that forces a much larger target to spend real money, time and credibility defending itself. The initiator spends attention; the target spends its balance sheet.

  • The reflexive loop

    Engagement algorithms promote the story, AI tools summarize it, traders react to the price moves it causes, and those moves seem to confirm the story. A modest start can become a cascade.

03 / RECOGNIZE THE PATTERN

How a campaign unfolds.

  1. 01 / MAP

    Find the weak point

    Capital structure, upcoming deadlines and where confidence is thinnest

  2. 02 / SHAPE

    Build the story

    A risk narrative close enough to the facts to be believed

  3. 03 / SEED

    Spread it quietly

    Many sources that look independent, so no single one can be answered

  4. 04 / AMPLIFY

    Let the loop work

    Timed to earnings, roadshows and launches, when attention is highest

Knowing these four steps is the best defense. Each one leaves signals that can be spotted early, before algorithms and markets take over.

04 / WHEN IT MATTERS MOST

Moments of exposure.

  • IPOs and capital raises

    Roadshows and pricing windows, when doubt can thin demand and lower valuations.

  • Product launches

    The weeks around a launch, when early negative signals get amplified and positive coverage gets buried.

  • Earnings and refinancing

    Results days and debt deadlines, when a risk story can raise the cost of money overnight.

  • Leadership and deals

    Leadership changes, mergers and acquisitions, when confidence in people and plans is easiest to shake.

  • Digital assets

    Token launches and trading venues, where pump-and-dump and rug-pull schemes run the same playbook at high speed.

  • Strategic industries

    Companies and sectors that matter to national economies, where financial pressure can replace other forms of conflict.

The thinking behind it

STRATEGY

Clausewitz on friction and the center of gravity, which in markets is investor and customer confidence. Machiavelli on appearance and timing. Churchill on morale as a strategic resource.

INFORMATION

Soviet Reflexive Control Theory, which studies how a prepared message leads someone to a decision they believe is their own, and the propaganda methods of the twentieth century, including protracted, many-voiced campaigns.

ECONOMICS

Long-cycle and economic-sovereignty thinking from Kondratiev, Glazyev, Katasonov and Delyagin: markets inflated by speculation are most sensitive to stories, and organizations that control their own funding and settlement are most resilient.

HISTORY

From the first recorded bear raid on the Dutch East India Company in 1609, through the rumor-driven collapses of 2008, to modern activist short campaigns and crypto schemes, the same pattern repeats.
